# Clarifications

Q: The brief lists guest checkout as a key feature and target user, but our project convention puts guest carts out of scope for the first release unless the PRD says otherwise. Should guest checkout/guest carts be included in the MVP, or should all purchases require a signed-in account?
A: Guest checkout is in the MVP. The brief names guest shoppers as a target user and new shoppers from social and search ads need fast, simple checkout, so please treat this as the PRD override to your convention. Guests can browse, add to a cart stored on the device and check out with just an email address (for the order confirmation) and a delivery address. After the order we will offer them the option to create an account. Guest carts do not need to sync with the website; only signed-in carts sync. Guests get order confirmation by email and a tracking link, but no in-app order history.
Q: Which sign-in methods are required in the MVP: email+password only, or also phone number, Apple and Google sign-in? (Apple sign-in is mandatory on iOS if any other third-party social login is offered.)
A: MVP sign-in methods are email and password, Apple sign-in and Google sign-in. Apple sign-in is required on iOS because we offer Google. Phone number sign-in (SMS one-time code) is deferred to a later release to save budget and SMS costs. Password reset by email is required, and account deletion must be available in the app for privacy compliance and App Store rules.
Q: Which payment methods are in the MVP? Our standard is Stripe PaymentIntents in test mode with cards; should Apple Pay, Google Pay and PayPal be included in the MVP or deferred, and are gift cards and buy-now-pay-later in or out of scope?
A: Use Stripe as the certified payment provider, with PaymentIntents. Build and test in test mode, then switch to live keys for launch. MVP payment methods: credit/debit cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay (these drive mobile conversion and come through Stripe with little extra work). PayPal is deferred to a fast-follow release. Gift cards are deferred. Buy-now-pay-later is out of scope for now. We never store card data ourselves, so we stay within PCI-DSS scope via Stripe.
Q: Which country, currency and language are supported at launch? Please confirm a single currency (ISO 4217 code) and single language for the MVP, with multi-currency and multi-language only as architectural readiness.
A: Launch in a single home market with a single currency and a single language. Our home market is the United States, so the currency is USD (ISO 4217) and the language is English (US). Multi-currency and multi-language are architectural readiness only: no hard-coded text or currency symbols, and strings and prices structured so we can add more later. We will ship domestically only at launch, with no international shipping.
Q: Which delivery options are in the MVP (standard, express, click-and-collect)? What are the shipping rates (flat, weight-based, or carrier-calculated), is there a free-shipping threshold, and how are delivery date estimates produced?
A: MVP delivery options: standard, express and in-store click-and-collect. Rates are flat and simple: standard $5.99 (3-5 business days), express $14.99 (1-2 business days), and click-and-collect is free. Free standard shipping on orders of $75 or more. No weight-based or live carrier-calculated rates in the MVP. Delivery date estimates are rule-based: the order cut-off time plus the fixed business-day range for the chosen option, shown as a date range at checkout. Click-and-collect is offered only when the selected store has stock, and customers are notified when the order is ready for pickup. Carrier tracking links come from our existing fulfillment carrier, with status updates pulled from the fulfillment system where possible.
Q: What is the returns policy for the MVP: return window, who pays return shipping, refund method (original payment via Stripe vs store credit), and are partial refunds and exchanges allowed? Is the return request started in-app in the MVP or handled by support?
A: Return window is 30 days from delivery for unused items in original condition. The customer pays return shipping unless the item is faulty or we sent the wrong item, in which case we pay. Refunds go back to the original payment method through Stripe; there is no store credit in the MVP. Partial refunds are allowed, since customers can return individual items from an order. Exchanges are not handled as a flow; customers return the item and place a new order. The return request is started in the app by logged-in customers: they choose the order, items, a reason and optionally a photo, and get a confirmation and status updates. Staff approve and trigger refunds in the admin back office. Guests can start a return through the contact form or a link in their confirmation email.
Q: Which loyalty features are in the MVP, if any? If included, what are the earn rate, redemption thresholds, tiers and expiry, and is it shared with in-store/POS purchases?
A: A simple loyalty program is in the MVP, but the full tier system is deferred. MVP: points only, earning 1 point per $1 spent on online orders, with 100 points = $1 reward, redeemable in $5 increments (500 points minimum) as a discount at checkout. Points are awarded after the return window closes or are reversed on returned items. Points expire after 12 months of account inactivity. Customers see their balance and history in the app. Tiers and member-only offers are phase 2. Sharing with in-store/POS purchases is also phase 2; at launch points are earned on app and website purchases only for signed-in accounts, and guests do not earn points.
Q: Which existing system (POS/inventory/ERP) is the source of truth for stock and prices, and what integration capability does it offer (API, file export, none)? Should the app sync stock and prices from it, and how frequently?
A: Our existing POS/inventory system is the source of truth for stock and prices, and the app must not become a second place where these are edited day to day. I am not technical, but I understand it offers a standard API and also scheduled CSV exports as a fallback; the vendor should confirm this during discovery in the first weeks. The app should sync stock and prices from it: stock roughly every 5 to 15 minutes, and prices at least hourly and whenever a change is made. Stock must be re-checked at checkout to avoid overselling. Product descriptions, images, banners and promotions are managed in the app's admin back office. Orders placed in the app must be sent to the fulfillment/POS system.
Q: Which push notification types are in the MVP: order status updates only, or also back-in-stock, price drops, abandoned cart reminders and promotions? Is consent opt-in per type?
A: MVP push notifications: order status updates (order confirmed, shipped, out for delivery, ready for pickup, delivered, return and refund updates), plus abandoned cart reminders and promotions. Back-in-stock and price-drop alerts are fast-follow in phase 2, tied to the wishlist. Consent is opt-in and per type: transactional order updates are on by default once the user allows push permission, while marketing types (abandoned cart and promotions) are off until the user explicitly opts in. Users can change these in settings at any time. Abandoned cart reminders are limited to a maximum of one or two per cart, and promotions to a small number per week.
Q: Is the admin back office in MVP scope, and if so which functions are required (product/price/inventory management, order management and status updates, refunds, promotions/promo codes, banners, review moderation, reports)? Which staff roles and permissions are needed, including support agents?
A: Yes, the admin back office is in the MVP because we have no technical staff and must run the app ourselves. Required functions: product content management (titles, descriptions, images, categories, variants), with price and stock read from the POS/inventory sync, plus manual override only for emergencies; order management and status updates; return approval and refunds through Stripe; promo codes; home screen banners and featured products; review moderation; and basic sales reports (orders, revenue, top products, promo code usage). Roles: Admin (full access, including user management), Catalog/Marketing Manager (products, banners, promotions, reviews), Operations (orders, returns, pickup status) and Support Agent (view orders and customers, issue refunds up to a set limit, no catalog or pricing changes). Every admin user signs in with their own login, and sensitive actions such as refunds are logged.
Q: Which customer support channels are in the MVP: FAQ/help center and contact form only, or also live chat/chatbot? Should we assume live chat is deferred?
A: Yes, assume live chat and chatbot are deferred. MVP support is an in-app help center/FAQ plus a contact form that sends to our support email or helpdesk inbox, with an optional link to an order from the customer's history. We aim to reply within one business day. Live chat or chatbot handoff will be considered after launch once we see contact volumes.
Q: Are promo codes in the MVP, and if so what rules apply (percentage or fixed amount, minimum spend, expiry, usage limits, one per order, stackability)?
A: Yes, promo codes are in the MVP. Rules: a code can be a percentage or a fixed dollar amount off the order; an optional minimum spend; start and expiry dates; an optional total usage limit and an optional limit of one use per customer; only one code per order (no stacking); and codes do not apply to shipping costs or gift cards. Whether they can apply to already-discounted items is a per-code setting, defaulting to not applying. Staff create and deactivate codes in the admin back office. Loyalty point redemption counts as a separate discount, and the vendor should confirm how it combines with a promo code. My default is that they can be combined but the total discount may never exceed the item subtotal.
Q: Are ratings and reviews in the MVP, and who can post them (verified purchasers only?) and is moderation required? Are wishlist, store locator with store stock check, recently viewed and personalized home/recommendations in the MVP or deferred?
A: Ratings and reviews are in the MVP. Only verified purchasers who are signed in can post a star rating and a short written review, and all reviews are moderated (approved by staff in the admin) before they show. Customers see average ratings and reviews on product pages. Other items: the wishlist (saved items) is in the MVP for signed-in users. Recently viewed is in the MVP because it is cheap to build. The store locator is in the MVP with store address, hours, phone and map directions. Store stock check is in the MVP at a simple level, showing in stock or out of stock per store based on the inventory sync, which also supports click-and-collect. Personalized home is limited in the MVP to a home screen managed by us with banners, featured products, seasonal campaigns, new arrivals and best sellers, plus recently viewed. AI and behavioral recommendations are deferred.
Q: Should cart and wishlist sync with an existing website account system, and is there an existing customer database or login to migrate or integrate with, or is this a new user base?
A: Yes, for signed-in customers the cart and wishlist should sync between the app and our website, since the brief requires it. We have an existing website with customer accounts, so the app should use the same customer accounts and order history, and existing customers should be able to log in with their current email and password with no new registration. The vendor needs to confirm with our website platform how to integrate authentication, for example through its customer API. If that turns out not to be feasible in the budget, the fallback is a shared account system where existing customers reset their password once on first app login. I am not aware of a need to migrate data beyond that. Social sign-in accounts must link to an existing account with the same email address, and we must not create duplicates.
Q: Are any products age-restricted or regulated (requiring age verification or shipping restrictions)? Also, how large is the launch catalog (products and variants), and does the app need to handle variant types such as size and color?
A: No, none of our products are age-restricted or regulated, so no age verification or shipping restrictions are needed at launch. The launch catalog is about 1,500 products and up to about 6,000 variants in total, all already on our website and POS. Yes, the app must handle variants, specifically size and color, with stock and sometimes price per variant, and multiple images per product with color-specific images where available. We will provide product content, descriptions and photos from our existing website and POS data, and our in-house team will prepare content for new products using the admin.
Q: Native (Swift/Kotlin) or cross-platform approach, and what are the minimum supported iOS and Android versions and the launch deadline/budget ceiling that should drive MVP scope?
A: We prefer a cross-platform approach, such as React Native or Flutter, to keep cost and time down with one team building both iOS and Android; the vendor should recommend the best fit and explain the trade-offs, and we would accept native only if there is a clear performance need. It must perform well on mid-range phones and slow connections. Minimum versions: support roughly the last three to four major OS versions, for example iOS 16 and later and Android 9 (API 28) and later, adjusted to cover about 95% of our current customers' devices once the vendor checks our website analytics. Deadline: MVP live in about 6 months, with a small beta of a few hundred customers first, then a phased rollout, ideally ahead of the next peak shopping season. Budget: I am setting a ceiling of $150,000 for the MVP build, including the admin back office, integrations and launch support. Separately, I expect an ongoing budget of about $3,000 to $5,000 per month for maintenance, hosting and third-party services, and the vendor must provide a transparent, predictable breakdown. If scope does not fit, cut in this order: PayPal, tiers, live chat and recommendations. Protect checkout, sync with the POS, the admin and the privacy and accessibility requirements.
