# Clarifications

Q: Which country and currency will we launch in first (one ISO 4217 currency, English only)? Which delivery partners, shipping rates and delivery time estimates apply, and is the free-shipping threshold a specific amount?
A: We launch in the United States only, in USD, English only. Delivery will be through USPS and UPS with a flat shipping rate of $5.95 for standard delivery (3-5 business days) and $12.95 for express (1-2 business days). Free standard shipping on orders of $75 or more. Returns shipping is covered below. Other countries and currencies come in a later phase.
Q: Project conventions say guest carts are out of scope unless the PRD says otherwise, but the brief asks for guest checkout. Do you want guest checkout in the MVP (requires guest carts and orders without an account), or should checkout require a signed-in user?
A: Yes, we want guest checkout in the MVP. The brief explicitly asks for it, and it matters for conversion, especially for gift buyers and older customers who want simple checkout. Please treat this as an explicit exception to the project convention: guest carts and guest orders (email required for confirmation and tracking) are in scope. After checkout we offer an optional prompt to create an account.
Q: Which sign-in methods are required at MVP: email and password, phone number (OTP), Apple login, Google login? Apple requires Sign in with Apple if other social logins are offered on iOS. Which of these can be deferred?
A: MVP: email and password, Sign in with Apple, and Google login. Since we offer Google login, we must offer Apple on iOS. Phone number (OTP) login is deferred to a later phase because of SMS costs and extra complexity. Phone number can still be collected as a contact field for delivery.
Q: Is cash on delivery offered at launch? If yes, in which areas, and how should stock reservation and order status work for COD orders, given the standard flow is pending_payment -> paid via Stripe webhook?
A: No cash on delivery at launch. We are launching in the US where it is not standard and it adds operational risk. All orders are paid online, so the standard flow pending_payment -> paid via the Stripe webhook applies and no COD-specific status or stock handling is needed. COD can be reconsidered when we expand to markets where it is common.
Q: Payments are planned on Stripe PaymentIntents in test mode. Are Apple Pay and Google Pay in the MVP, and are local wallets and saved payment methods (Stripe saved cards) in or out of the MVP? If local wallets are in, which ones?
A: Stripe is our single payment provider. In the MVP: card payments, Apple Pay and Google Pay are in. Local wallets are out of the MVP for the US launch (no additional wallets such as PayPal for now). Saved payment methods (Stripe saved cards for signed-in users) are in at MVP, since they support repeat purchases, but we never store card data ourselves. Test mode is fine during development, with live mode switched on for launch.
Q: What is the exact returns policy: return window in days, who pays return shipping, refund to original payment vs store credit, are exchanges (size swaps) supported at MVP, and which items are non-returnable? Should refunds be issued automatically through Stripe or manually by staff?
A: Return window is 30 days from delivery for unworn items with tags attached. Return shipping is free for size exchanges and for faulty or wrong items; for other returns the customer pays a flat $5.95 deducted from the refund. Refunds go to the original payment method (no store credit at MVP). Size exchanges are supported at MVP and initiated in the app. Non-returnable: items marked final sale in clearance, and worn, washed or damaged items. Return requests are started in the app, staff approve them in the back office, and staff trigger the refund manually from the back office once the return is received and checked, which calls Stripe to issue it. Automatic refunds are not needed at MVP.
Q: Is the admin back office built from scratch or on an existing platform such as Shopify? What roles and permissions are needed (e.g. admin, catalog manager, fulfilment/support), and what must the basic sales reports show?
A: We want a simple custom admin back office as part of this project, per the project setup, and it must be easy for non-technical staff. We are not moving to Shopify for the MVP. Roles at MVP: Admin (full access including users, promotions, reports), Catalog Manager (products, prices, images, stock view), and Fulfilment/Support (orders, returns, refunds requests). Basic sales reports: sales by day/week/month (orders, revenue, average order value), best-selling shirts, orders by status, returns count and return rate, and promo code usage. Export to CSV is a nice-to-have.
Q: What is the integration method with the existing inventory/stock system (API, file sync, database)? Is it the source of truth for stock per variant (size/color), how often does it sync, and who owns the product catalog data?
A: Our existing inventory system is the source of truth for stock per variant (size and color). It offers a REST API, and the app backend should sync stock from it every 15 minutes, with a live stock check at checkout before payment. Our team owns the product catalog data (descriptions, photos, fabric, fit, prices) and will load the initial 150-200 shirts; staff can edit them in the back office. The SKU from the inventory system is the key that links variants. The app never overwrites stock in the inventory system except to report sold quantities or reservations as the API allows.
Q: Which promotion types are required at MVP: percentage off, fixed amount, free shipping threshold, seasonal sale sections? Can codes be stacked, and are there usage limits, minimum spend or expiry rules?
A: Required at MVP: percentage-off codes, fixed-amount codes, the free shipping threshold ($75), and seasonal sale sections (products flagged as on sale with a sale price). Codes cannot be stacked: one code per order, though the free shipping threshold still applies alongside a code. Each code can have an expiry date, an optional minimum spend, and an optional total usage limit plus one use per customer. Sale prices and codes can combine unless a product is marked as excluded.
Q: Should customer reviews be supported at MVP? If yes, can only verified purchasers review, is moderation required, and who moderates?
A: Yes, basic reviews are in the MVP because they help buyers feel confident about fit and quality: star rating 1-5 plus short text, and a fit feedback option (runs small / true to size / runs large). Only verified purchasers who received the item can post. All reviews are moderated before they appear, and our operations team does it from the back office (approve or reject). Photo reviews and replies are later.
Q: Fit helper: should the recommendation rules (height, weight, preferred fit -> size) be supplied by your team, and do you have size charts per product or per brand?
A: Yes, our team will supply the recommendation rules as a simple table mapping height, weight and preferred fit (slim, regular, relaxed) to a size. We have size charts per fit type (slim, regular, relaxed) rather than per individual product, and all our shirts are our own brand so there is one set of charts. Chart measurements are in inches with cm shown too. The rules should be configurable by staff later, but for MVP it's fine if the developers load them as data we provide.
Q: Which push notifications are in MVP (order updates, abandoned cart, new arrivals, back-in-stock), and which email or SMS channels should be used for order confirmations? What are the opt-in and frequency rules?
A: MVP push notifications: order updates (confirmed, shipped, delivered, return status), back-in-stock alerts for items on a user's wishlist or alert list, and abandoned cart reminders (one reminder about 24 hours after abandonment). New-arrival pushes are included but sent manually by staff, with a cap of 1 marketing push per week. Marketing pushes (abandoned cart, new arrivals) require opt-in; order updates are transactional. Order confirmations and shipping notices go by email; no SMS at MVP. Users can manage notification preferences in the app settings.
Q: Which privacy regime applies in the launch market (GDPR, CCPA, other)? Do we need consent banners, data export, and account deletion with a specified retention period for order records?
A: With a US launch, CCPA/CPRA applies mainly (California customers), and we want to be compliant across the board, using GDPR-style good practice so we can expand to Europe later. We need: a privacy policy and terms linked in the app, a clear consent prompt for analytics/marketing tracking and push notifications, in-app account deletion (required by Apple), and a way for users to request a copy of their data (can be by email request handled by staff at MVP). On deletion we remove personal data but keep anonymized order records for 7 years for tax and accounting purposes. We do not sell personal data.
Q: What is the budget ceiling and the target launch date, including any seasonal deadline? Which items (loyalty program, live chat, made-to-measure, other products) are explicitly out of the MVP, and what customer support channel at launch (email/phone only)?
A: Budget ceiling for the MVP is $60,000 USD, consistent with the mid five-figure budget. Target launch is 4-5 months from project start; ideally we go live in time for the start of the holiday sales season, before mid-November, but the quality of the launch matters more than hitting a specific day. Explicitly out of the MVP: loyalty/rewards program, live chat, made-to-measure or tailoring, other products such as trousers and accessories (though the design and data model should not prevent adding them later), phone OTP login, cash on delivery, multi-country and multi-currency. Customer support at launch is email and phone only, with contact details and an FAQ in the app.
Q: Which analytics tool do you want to use, and what are the target minimum supported OS versions and performance targets (e.g. app start and product list load time) for the MVP?
A: Use Firebase Analytics (Google Analytics for Firebase) with crash reporting through Firebase Crashlytics, as it's free and works on both platforms. We need to track the funnel: product views, add to cart, checkout started, purchase, and drop-off points, plus best sellers. Minimum OS support: iOS 15 and above, Android 8.0 (API 26) and above. Performance targets on an average 4G connection: app cold start under 3 seconds, product list first load under 2 seconds, product detail under 2 seconds, with images lazily loaded and optimized. Accessibility should follow standard practice: screen reader labels, dynamic text sizing, and sufficient color contrast (WCAG AA).
Q: How should US sales tax be handled at launch? For example, should we calculate tax by destination state using Stripe Tax or another tax service, charge a flat rate, or show prices tax-inclusive? Which states is the business required to collect tax in?
A: We will launch in the US with prices shown excluding tax, as is normal for US retail. Sales tax is calculated at checkout by destination, and shown as a separate line before the customer pays. We do not want a flat rate, because it would be wrong in many states and create compliance risk. Please use an automated tax service rather than building our own tax tables. Stripe Tax is our preferred choice if Stripe is our payment provider, since it keeps fees and integration simple. Another service is fine if it is cheaper or easier to integrate, but the decision should not add significant cost or delay. Regarding which states we must collect in: the brief does not specify this, and I am not a tax expert. Our accountant will confirm our nexus (where we are required to collect) before launch. Our working assumption is that we collect in our home state and in any other state where we have a physical presence or exceed the economic nexus thresholds. To keep this flexible, the tax configuration should be something our operations team can update without a developer, for example by adding or removing states as registrations change. Tax should be calculated per order and itemized on receipts and in order history. Refunds and returns must reverse the tax correctly. Tax-exempt and wholesale customers are out of scope for the MVP.
Q: Which US delivery destinations are supported at launch: all 50 states, or only the contiguous 48? Are PO boxes, APO/FPO addresses, and Alaska/Hawaii/US territories supported, and do the flat rates of $5.95 standard and $12.95 express apply to them unchanged?
A: At launch we ship to the contiguous 48 states only, plus Washington DC. We will not ship to Alaska, Hawaii, or US territories (Puerto Rico, Guam, etc.) in the first release, because the costs and delivery times are different and we do not want to lose margin on those orders. Keep this as a configurable list so we can add them later. PO boxes are supported for standard shipping only, because our carrier's express service does not deliver to PO boxes. APO/FPO/DPO military addresses are not supported at launch. The address form should validate addresses and show a clear message if a customer enters an unsupported destination, and this should happen before checkout and not at payment. The flat rates of $5.95 standard and $12.95 express apply unchanged to all supported destinations in the contiguous 48 states and DC, including PO boxes for standard. We will separately confirm the free-shipping threshold from the brief, and when it applies it covers standard shipping only.
