# Clarifications

Q: Guest checkout conflict: the brief lists guest checkout, but our project rules say a cart belongs to a signed-in user and guest carts are out of scope for the first release. Should guest checkout be included in the MVP (which would require changing that rule), or should all purchases require a signed-in account?
A: Follow your project rule: for the MVP, all purchases require a signed-in account. Guest checkout was in the brief to reduce drop-off, but I accept deferring it to a later release to keep scope and cost down. To limit drop-off, make sign-up as quick as possible (email with a simple code or link, no long forms), and let people browse the catalogue, search, and view product details without signing in. Ask them to sign in or sign up only when they add to cart or check out. Guest checkout goes on the post-launch list.
Q: Which single country and currency (ISO 4217 code) do we launch in, and what is the single launch language? This determines price formatting, tax/VAT display (prices tax-inclusive or exclusive?), address formats, phone number validation, and the applicable privacy law (GDPR or local equivalent).
A: We launch in one country only, the United Kingdom, with GBP as the currency and English (UK) as the only language. Prices are shown tax-inclusive (VAT included), as customers expect in UK retail. Use UK address formats with postcode lookup if it is cheap to add, and UK phone number validation. The applicable privacy regime is UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018. Other countries, currencies, and languages come later.
Q: Do we integrate with the existing product/stock/POS system at launch, or do staff upload and manage products and stock manually in the admin area? If integrated, what system is it, is the sync one-way (read-only into the app) or two-way, and how often does stock need to refresh?
A: We do not want to maintain inventory twice, but I also want to keep the MVP realistic. At launch, the app's admin area is the place where staff manage products, prices, and stock, with a bulk CSV import to load the initial catalogue. Live POS integration is phase two. I do not want a two-way sync in the MVP. When we do integrate later, it should be one-way from our existing stock system into the app for product and stock data, refreshing at least every 15 minutes, with orders being passed back in a later step. For launch, stock must decrease automatically when an app order is placed, and staff can adjust stock manually. Since in-store sales will not update the app automatically at launch, staff will do a simple daily stock check on fast-moving items.
Q: What are the delivery rules for the MVP: which delivery methods (home delivery, in-store pickup, or both), delivery fee structure (flat, free above a threshold, by zone), serviceable areas, and for pickup, which stores are available and how does a store mark an order ready for collection?
A: Both home delivery and in-store pickup are offered. Home delivery: a flat fee of £3.99, free for orders of £40 or more, to UK mainland addresses only (no islands, Northern Ireland, or international at launch). No zone-based pricing and no delivery time slots; show an estimated delivery of 2-4 working days. In-store pickup: free, and available from all our stores that hold stock, so the app needs a simple list of pickup stores that staff can maintain (I will provide the store names and addresses). Stock is treated as a single shared pool across stores for the MVP, not per store. Staff mark an order as Ready for collection in the admin area, which triggers a push notification and email to the customer, and then mark it Collected when handed over. Orders not collected within 7 days can be cancelled and refunded by staff.
Q: How should returns and refunds work at launch: is the app only expected to let staff issue a full or partial refund from the admin area (via Stripe) and move the order to refunded, or must customers be able to request returns in the app? What is the return window and policy?
A: Keep it simple: at launch the app does not need a customer-facing return request flow. Staff can issue a full or partial refund from the admin area via Stripe and set the order status to Refunded or Partially refunded. The app should show the returns policy as static text and tell customers to contact support by email to start a return. Return window is 30 days from delivery or collection for unused items in original condition. Customers pay return postage unless the item is faulty or wrong. In-app return requests come in a later release.
Q: Which staff roles and permissions are needed in the admin area (e.g., a single admin role vs. separate roles for catalogue/stock vs. order handling vs. reporting), approximately how many staff users, and should the admin be a web app or inside the mobile app?
A: We will have about 5-8 staff users at launch. Two roles are enough: Admin (full access, including products, prices, stock, orders, refunds, reporting, and staff user management; 1-2 people) and Staff (manage orders, update order statuses, and adjust stock; no price changes, no refunds, no reporting, no user management). The admin area should be a web app used on a laptop or desktop browser, not inside the customer mobile app, because it is cheaper and easier for staff to use for catalogue and order work. It should be mobile-browser friendly enough to check an order in a pinch, but that is not a priority.
Q: What is the expected launch catalogue size (number of products and variants such as size/colour), and who supplies product photos and descriptions? Is stock tracked per variant for all products?
A: Plan for about 300 products at launch, with the catalogue able to grow to around 1,000 without rework. Roughly a third of products have variants, mainly size and colour, averaging 3-5 variants each. Stock is tracked per variant where variants exist, and per product otherwise. My team will supply the product photos (at least one good photo per product, ideally 3-4), titles, descriptions, and prices, provided as a spreadsheet plus an image folder for the bulk import. The app team does not need to produce any content.
Q: What are the rules for push notifications: which order status changes trigger notifications, and are promotional notifications in scope for MVP (requiring opt-in consent and a campaign-sending tool for staff), or only transactional order notifications at launch?
A: Transactional notifications are in scope for launch: order confirmed, order dispatched (home delivery), out for delivery if we have that status, delivered, ready for collection (pickup), and refunded or cancelled. Promotional notifications are a business goal, so I want the capability in the MVP but kept minimal. That means an explicit opt-in prompt (off by default, with a settings toggle to change it at any time) and a very simple way for an Admin to send a single message to all opted-in users. No segmentation, scheduling, or automation at launch. Transactional notifications do not require marketing consent, but promotional ones do, in line with UK GDPR and PECR. If this threatens the timeline, I would accept dropping the promotional sending tool and keeping only the opt-in capture, as long as we can send promotions soon after launch.
Q: Sign-up method for MVP: email only, phone only, or both? Are social logins out of scope? Is email verification or phone OTP required before ordering?
A: Email only for the MVP, with a password, plus a password reset flow. Phone sign-up is out of scope for now, though we will collect a phone number at checkout for delivery contact. Social logins (Apple, Google, Facebook) are out of scope for launch. Email verification is required before the first order is placed, and a verification link or code is fine. No phone OTP is needed. If Apple's rules require Sign in with Apple, we would only add it if we introduced social login, which we are not doing.
Q: Apple Pay / Google Pay via Stripe: confirm these wallets are in scope for the MVP alongside card payments, and confirm that Stripe (test mode for this release) is the approved provider. Any other payment methods required (e.g., cash on delivery or pay in store)?
A: Confirmed: Stripe is the approved payment provider, and card payments plus Apple Pay and Google Pay are in scope for the MVP. Building and testing in Stripe test mode for this release is fine; we will move to live mode with our own Stripe account before public launch. The standard Stripe fees of around 1.5% + 20p for UK cards and 2.5% + 20p for non-UK cards are acceptable. No other payment methods are needed: no cash on delivery, no pay in store, no PayPal, and no buy-now-pay-later at launch. Pickup orders are also paid in the app in advance. We will not store any card details.
Q: Are loyalty/rewards, promo codes or discounts, and in-app customer support chat out of scope for the MVP? If a support channel is needed in the app, which one (email, phone, or chat)?
A: Yes, all three are out of scope for the MVP: no loyalty or rewards programme, no promo codes or discount engine, and no in-app chat. Any promotions in launch will be done simply by staff changing prices, plus promotional push messages. For support, we need a Help or Contact screen with our support email address (tap to open the email app), our phone number for order queries (tap to call) with opening hours, and our delivery and returns policy. Email is the primary channel. Loyalty and promo codes are candidates for version 2.
Q: Brand and accessibility: can you provide brand guidelines (logo, colours), and is there any accessibility standard required beyond platform basics (e.g., WCAG 2.1 AA, dynamic text size, screen reader support)?
A: Yes, I will provide our logo files, primary and secondary brand colours, and fonts, with a one-page brand guide. Until then, design with a clean, neutral look and make colours easy to swap. For accessibility, the target is the platform basics done properly rather than a formal certification: support dynamic text sizing, screen reader labels (VoiceOver and TalkBack) on all buttons, images, and form fields, sufficient colour contrast in line with WCAG 2.1 AA, and touch targets big enough to tap comfortably. A formal WCAG audit is not required for launch, but we should not build anything that blocks these standards.
