# Clarifications

Q: Which countries, currencies and languages must the MVP support at launch (the project stores prices as integer minor units with an ISO 4217 code, so we need the exact currency list), and how should tax/VAT be handled (prices tax-inclusive or exclusive, invoices required)?
A: Launch in one market only: the United Kingdom, with a single currency, GBP, and English only. The system should still store prices as minor units with a currency code so we can add EUR and other languages later, but nothing else is needed at launch. Prices are shown VAT-inclusive, as is normal for UK consumers, at the standard 20% rate (we have no reduced-rate items at launch). Customers need a VAT receipt or invoice: the order confirmation email should include an itemised receipt showing the VAT amount and our VAT number. Multi-region tax rules are out of scope for the MVP.
Q: The brief lists guest checkout, but project conventions say carts belong to signed-in users and guest carts are out of scope unless the PRD says otherwise. Should guest checkout be in the MVP, or is sign-in required before adding to cart/checkout?
A: Follow the project convention: for the MVP, customers must sign in (or create an account) before adding to cart and checking out. Browsing the catalogue and searching should work without an account. Guest checkout stays a business priority, but it moves to phase 2. To keep friction low, account creation must be very quick, with Apple and Google sign-in and no long forms.
Q: Which sign-in methods are required for the MVP: email+password only, or also phone number, Apple and Google sign-in (Apple sign-in is mandatory on iOS if any other social login is offered)?
A: MVP sign-in methods: email and password, plus Sign in with Apple and Google sign-in. Apple is required on iOS because we offer Google. Phone-number sign-in is deferred to a later phase. We need password reset by email and a basic profile with saved addresses.
Q: Which payment methods are in the MVP? Conventions assume Stripe PaymentIntents in test mode with card payments; should Apple Pay and Google Pay be included, and is cash on delivery required (it would need a different order/payment flow than pending_payment -> paid via webhook)?
A: Use Stripe as our payment provider. MVP payments are credit and debit cards, plus Apple Pay and Google Pay via Stripe, since one-tap wallets matter for mobile conversion. Build and test in Stripe test mode for now, with the switch to live mode at launch. Cash on delivery is not required and is out of scope, so the standard flow of pending payment to paid via webhook is fine. We will not store card details. Gift cards are not part of the MVP.
Q: Which delivery options are in the MVP: home delivery only (standard and express), or also click-and-collect? What are the delivery zones, shipping price rules, free-shipping threshold, and is live carrier rate/tracking integration needed or are flat rates and manual status updates enough?
A: MVP offers home delivery only, within mainland UK, with two options: Standard (3-5 working days) at a flat £3.95 and Express (next working day for orders before 2pm) at a flat £7.95. Standard shipping is free for orders of £50 or more. Excluded for now: Northern Ireland, the Highlands and Islands, and international delivery. Click-and-collect is phase 2. Flat rates and manual status updates in the back office are enough for launch: staff mark orders as processing, shipped (with a tracking number and link entered by hand) and delivered. Live carrier rate and tracking integration is later. The customer sees the shipping cost and estimated delivery date before paying.
Q: What is the return and refund policy (return window, who pays return shipping, refund to original payment method only, partial refunds), and should returns/refund requests be in-app in the MVP?
A: Returns window is 30 days from delivery for unused items in original condition. The customer pays return shipping unless the item is faulty or we sent the wrong item, in which case we pay. Refunds go back to the original payment method only, with no store credit at launch. Partial refunds are allowed, for example for one item in an order, and the original outbound shipping is refunded only if the whole order is returned or the item was faulty. In the MVP, customers can submit a return request in the app, choosing the order, item(s) and a reason. Staff review and approve it in the back office and trigger the refund through Stripe. Automated return labels are later.
Q: Does the existing inventory/ERP system have an API, and what sync model is required (real-time, periodic polling, or manual stock management in the admin back office for the MVP)? Which system is the source of truth for stock?
A: Our current stock system is a basic off-the-shelf inventory tool, and I can't confirm it has a usable API. The business decision is that the stock system stays the source of truth because it also covers our physical stores and warehouse. For the MVP, we accept periodic sync: stock levels should be imported automatically every 15 minutes if the API exists, or via a scheduled CSV import if it doesn't. The app should also reserve or decrement stock when an order is paid, and show low-stock or out-of-stock status, to avoid overselling. Manual stock adjustment in the admin back office must be available as a fallback. Real-time sync is a later improvement.
Q: Which of these are in scope for the MVP versus later phases: loyalty points, gift cards, promo codes, wishlist, customer reviews, personalized offers, live chat, abandoned-cart and back-in-stock push notifications?
A: In the MVP: promo codes (simple percentage or fixed-amount discount codes managed in the back office), order-update push notifications (confirmed, shipped, delivered) with opt-in controls, a wishlist (simple save for later for signed-in users), and an in-app FAQ with a contact form that goes to our support email. Phase 2: loyalty points, abandoned-cart reminders, back-in-stock alerts, customer reviews, and guest checkout. Phase 3 or later: gift cards, personalized offers, and live chat. Email and FAQ support are enough at launch. Marketing push notifications require explicit consent.
Q: Which admin back office capabilities are needed at MVP (product/variant/price/stock management, order status updates, refunds, customer lookup, promotions/banners, basic reports), and should there be distinct staff roles with different permissions (store manager, fulfilment, support)? Should we build a custom admin or use an off-the-shelf tool given there is no technical staff?
A: MVP back office must cover: product, variant, price and stock management (including photos and descriptions), order list with status updates and tracking number entry, refund and return handling, customer lookup with order history, promo code management, home-screen banners, and basic sales reports (sales by day, top products, orders count). Roles: three simple ones: Store Manager (full access), Fulfilment (orders and stock only), and Support (orders, customers, returns and refunds, no price changes). Because we have no technical staff, prefer a simple, easy-to-use admin, and I am open to an off-the-shelf or headless commerce admin tool if it meets these needs at lower cost and risk. Whatever is chosen must be usable without developer help.
Q: Are the products only our own (single seller), confirming that marketplace/third-party sellers are out of scope, and what are the target launch date and any hard budget ceiling that should drive MVP scope cuts?
A: Yes, only our own products, with a single seller. Marketplace and third-party sellers are out of scope. Target is an MVP launch on iOS and Android within about 6 months, so a launch date of around the end of March 2027, from today's date of 30 September 2026. We should use one cross-platform codebase. Budget is limited: my working ceiling is about £120,000 for the MVP build, including design, testing and store submission, plus a modest monthly running budget. If scope threatens that ceiling or the date, cut in this order: wishlist first, then promo codes, then Express delivery, while protecting the core journey of browse, sign in, cart, pay, and order tracking.
