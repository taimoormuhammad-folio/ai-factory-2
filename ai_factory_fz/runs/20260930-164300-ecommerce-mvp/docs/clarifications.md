# Clarifications

Q: Which single country/region do we ship to at launch, and which currency (ISO 4217 code) and language should the app use? Please also confirm whether prices shown include VAT/tax and whether invoices or receipts are required.
A: We ship to our home market only at launch, a single country. The brief does not name it, so we are going with Germany. The currency is EUR and the app is in one language, English, to keep version 1 simple. Localization can come later. Prices shown to customers include VAT, as is normal for consumer retail in the EU, and the VAT amount is shown as a line on the order summary. An order confirmation email serves as the receipt, and it must show the items, prices, VAT amount, shipping, total and order number. A formal PDF invoice is not needed in version 1. Our team can issue invoices manually if a customer asks.
Q: Is guest checkout in or out of version 1? Our default assumption is that carts and orders belong to signed-in users only (no guest checkout), and that sign-in is email and password only with no social login. Please confirm or correct.
A: Confirmed. Guest checkout is out of version 1. Carts and orders belong to signed-in users, and customers create an account with email and password, including password reset. There is no social login (Google or Apple) in version 1, which also means Sign in with Apple is not required. We will review guest checkout and social sign-in in a later release, based on cart abandonment data.
Q: What delivery options do we offer at launch (for example one flat rate versus standard and express)? What are the shipping prices and estimated delivery times, and is there free shipping above a certain order value?
A: We offer two options at launch. Standard delivery costs EUR 4.90 and takes 3-5 working days. Express delivery costs EUR 9.90 and takes 1-2 working days. Shipping is free on standard delivery for orders of EUR 60 or more. Express always carries its charge. These prices and thresholds should be configurable by admins, or at least easy for developers to change, without a new app release. Delivery is to the home country only.
Q: What is the returns and refund policy, and is a return flow in the app part of version 1? Our default assumption is that refunds are handled manually by admins, and that the order is marked refunded after the refund is issued in Stripe. Please confirm or correct.
A: Confirmed. Our policy is that customers can return unworn items with tags within 30 days of delivery for a full refund of the item price. Return shipping is paid by the customer unless the item is faulty. There is no in-app return flow in version 1. Customers contact our support by email, and the returns policy text is shown in the app as a static page. Admins issue refunds manually in Stripe and then mark the order as Refunded in the back-office. Partial refunds are handled manually in Stripe as well. An in-app return request is a candidate for a later release.
Q: Are card payments through Stripe (test mode for the first release, using PaymentIntents) acceptable as the only payment method in version 1, with Apple Pay, Google Pay and PayPal deferred to a later release?
A: Yes, Stripe card payments are the only payment method in version 1, and Apple Pay, Google Pay and PayPal are deferred to a later release. Stripe test mode is fine for development and testing. Before public launch we need to switch to live mode with our real Stripe account, so the live keys must be a simple configuration change. We only want to go live once the full test is passed. Using PaymentIntents is fine, as the technical choice is up to the team. We never store card details ourselves. When we expand, the first addition is Apple Pay and Google Pay, then PayPal if customers ask for it.
Q: Should order status notifications be email only in version 1, with no push notifications? Which emails are required (order confirmation, shipped, delivered, cancelled, password reset)?
A: Yes, email only in version 1 with no push notifications. The required emails are order confirmation, shipped, delivered, cancelled and password reset. We also want a refund confirmation email when an order is marked Refunded. Emails should be simple, carry our branding and show the order number and status. Push notifications can come in a later release.
Q: How is the product and stock data loaded at launch: admin CSV/spreadsheet import, manual entry in the back-office, or both? How often does stock need to be updated from the physical shops, and is there a single source of truth?
A: Both. We will load the initial catalog of a few hundred products through a CSV/spreadsheet import in the back-office. Day-to-day changes such as editing a product, photos, price or stock are done manually in the back-office by non-technical staff. Stock is updated from the physical shop by importing a stock spreadsheet once a day, in the morning, and staff can correct individual items manually at any time. The back-office is the single source of truth for what the app shows, and our existing stock system is the source we export from. There is no live integration with the shop systems in version 1. Stock is reduced when an order is paid, so the app does not oversell online items.
Q: Are there multiple admin roles with different permissions (for example catalog manager versus order processor), or one admin role? Should admins be able to cancel orders and trigger refunds?
A: One admin role in version 1, with full access, because our team is small. Admin accounts are created by us and are separate from shopper accounts, with secure sign-in. Admins can manage products, photos, prices and stock, update order statuses, cancel orders, and trigger or record refunds. Separate roles such as catalog manager and order processor can be added later. The back-office should log which admin made each status change to keep an audit trail.
Q: The brief lists order statuses as Placed, Processing, Shipped, Delivered and Cancelled, while our standard flow is pending_payment, paid, fulfilled, delivered, cancelled and refunded. Can we map them as: Placed = pending_payment/paid, Processing = paid, Shipped = fulfilled, Delivered = delivered, and show Cancelled and Refunded as is? Is this acceptable?
A: Mostly yes, with one correction so customers are not confused. Customers should see one clear status at a time. Placed is shown when the order is created and payment is pending or just confirmed (pending_payment, then paid). Processing is shown once an admin starts preparing the order after payment (paid and picked up by an admin). Shipped = fulfilled, Delivered = delivered, and Cancelled and Refunded are shown as is. A plain paid order should show as Placed until an admin moves it to Processing. Unpaid orders that expire are shown as Cancelled. Adding Refunded to the customer-facing list is fine.
Q: How long should stock stay reserved during checkout before an unpaid order expires and releases stock (for example 15 or 30 minutes)?
A: 30 minutes. After that, an unpaid order expires, is marked Cancelled and the stock is released. If the customer pays after the order has expired, the team should handle it as a rare exception, for example by checking whether stock is still available and otherwise refunding. Stock is not reserved just by adding an item to the cart, only once checkout starts.
Q: What is the target launch date, and which success metrics matter most in the first three months (for example conversion rate, cart abandonment, repeat purchase rate), so we can define the analytics events to track?
A: The target is a first working version live on both Android and iOS about 3 months from now, so around the end of December 2026, ideally before the peak of the winter shopping season. A phased plan is fine and we have no fixed budget figure yet. The most important success metrics for the first three months are, in order: 1) conversion rate from app visit to completed order, 2) cart abandonment rate and where customers drop off in checkout, 3) repeat purchase rate, and 4) number of registered accounts. Downloads are useful but secondary. Please track these events: app open, sign-up, sign-in, search (with keyword), category view, product view, add to cart, remove from cart, checkout started, payment succeeded or failed, and order placed. The analytics must respect GDPR, including consent where required.
